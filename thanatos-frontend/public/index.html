The Ritual

Death is not the end of a token.

I

Verify

Point the altar at a dead token. The verifier checks it once had a market on Robinhood Chain and now trades under 5% of its peak, then signs a one-hour voucher.

II

Sacrifice

Burn it forever for 0.0005 ETH. Verified dead earns 38 karma ×1.5 plus an amount bonus and bends the clock; unverified tokens earn 5, capped at 25 per wallet per epoch.

III

Seal

When the death clock runs out the altar seals — never sooner than six hours in, and only with at least 0.01 ETH in the treasury. A thin treasury extends the epoch instead.

IV

Rebirth

20% to the protocol, then 30 / 40 / 30 of the rest: fee share to soul holders, a new token launched on Pons and airdropped to every burner, a $THANATOS buyback-burn.

Necromancer Protocol · Robinhood Chain

The treasury holds ETH from two sources: a 0.0005 ETH altar fee on every offering, and 2.7% of the trading volume of every token the altar has reborn, collected from the Pons fee escrow. Dead tokens add no ETH; they add soul weight. At rebirth, 20% goes to the protocol and the rest is split 30 / 40 / 30 — fee share to soul holders, a seed buy of the new token airdropped to every burner, a $THANATOS buyback-burn. An epoch never seals before six hours or with less than 0.01 ETH in the treasury. $THANATOS itself pays its 2.7% to holders through Pons Holder Fee Sharing; the altar never touches it. If nobody offers and nobody trades, the treasury does not grow. Fee share is not yield and is not promised.
